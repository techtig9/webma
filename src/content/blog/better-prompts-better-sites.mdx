The single biggest factor in how good your generated site turns out is how much the AI actually knows about what you're building. A few extra sentences up front save several rounds of edits later.

## 1. Say who the site is for

"A landing page" and "a landing page for freelance wedding photographers booking spring 2027 clients" produce very different results. Name your audience.

## 2. Name the one thing you want a visitor to do

Every generated page gets a stronger call-to-action when you're explicit about the goal — book a call, join a waitlist, buy a product, read your work.

## 3. Mention your industry's conventions, if you have strong opinions

If you already know you want a photography portfolio to be image-first with minimal text, or a SaaS landing page to lead with a demo screenshot, say so. The AI will follow real conventions by default, but it can't read your specific taste.

## 4. Use the follow-up questions

After your first description, webma asks a short set of follow-up questions about theme, color, and style. Treat them as part of the prompt, not a formality — they materially change the output.

## 5. Iterate with targeted edits, not full regenerations

Once you have a site you mostly like, use section-level AI editing to fix specific things rather than regenerating the whole site from scratch. You'll keep everything that's already working.
